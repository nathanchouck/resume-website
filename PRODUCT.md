# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: aerospace industry recruiters and hiring engineers evaluating Nathan for internships and early-career roles. They arrive with a résumé-scanner's patience and leave with either a download or nothing.

Secondary: readers who come for the writing — the Musings essays, stories, and poems. The site should reward them, not just tolerate them; the writing is part of the case, not a side room.

## Product Purpose

nathanhouck.com is Nathan Houck's personal portfolio: an aerospace engineering student at Embry-Riddle (Astronautics concentration, class of 2027) presenting his work, trajectory, and writing. The content story is shifting: away from the wind-tunnel / experimental-aerodynamics framing, toward the space side — rocket propulsion and nozzle theory (a cold gas nozzle experiment is underway in summer 2026) — alongside substantial work on agentic AI memory systems, and the writing. Success is a recruiter downloading the résumé because the depth and trajectory convinced them, and a reader remembering who made this.

## Positioning

An aerospace student working on rocket propulsion who also builds AI memory systems and publishes real essays — the range is the claim. Every page should reinforce that these three crafts belong to one careful person.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Download résumé (filled, unambiguous). Secondary: email as a quiet text link — no forced choice between two equal buttons.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "An engineer who writes as carefully as he builds."
- Belief ladder: (1) This student has real propulsion depth for an undergrad. (2) His trajectory — space plus AI — matches where the industry is going. (3) He would fit my team now.
- Proof on hand: deferred by Nathan's request (July 2026). The cold gas nozzle experiment and the EPI-USE AI internship are producing proof this summer; collect artifacts into `.impeccable/assets/proof/` when ready. Until then, specificity in copy (temperatures, part counts, airfoil designations) carries the proof burden.

## Brand Personality

Curious, rigorous, warm. Understated confidence: specific numbers and well-made sentences do the talking, never superlatives. Two voices, one person — the engineer (precise, metadata, measurements) and the writer (essays, the deliberate "No artificial intelligence tools were used in the writing of this essay" line, the Dune epigraph). Generic student-speak ("passionate about aerospace") is off-voice; name the actual thing.

## Anti-references

- LinkedIn / the generic student portfolio: centered circular headshot, job-title-first, equally-weighted bullet lists.
- Bright SaaS marketing: gradient heroes, metric cards, salesy CTAs.
- AI-template aesthetics: cream-and-serif editorial defaults, eyebrow labels on every section, glassmorphism — anything that reads "generated."
- Sci-fi / space kitsch: starfields, planet illustrations, NASA-punk styling.

The kitsch ban is not a ban on the subject. Nathan explicitly wants space and AI presence in the design — rendered as engineering artifacts (a nozzle contour, a technical drawing, telemetry-style metadata, a memory-graph figure), the kind of thing that comes off a propulsion engineer's desk, never movie-set decoration.

## Design Principles

1. **Lead with the work, not the title.** "Aerospace Engineering Student" is what the school calls him; the pages open with what he built, measured, or wrote.
2. **Specificity is credibility.** Real numbers, real designations, real dates — bolded where they carry the proof. If a sentence would survive on any student's site, rewrite it.
3. **The engineer's desk, not the movie set.** Space and AI show up as authentic technical artifacts, integrated into the system of the page — never themed decoration.
4. **The writing is a feature.** Musings is the most distinctive thing here; treat it as a first-class destination with its own editorial care.
5. **Quiet confidence.** Warmth and curiosity over polish and salesmanship. The site convinces by being well-made, the way the work is.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text contrast ≥4.5:1, keyboard navigable with visible focus (accent focus ring already in place), and `prefers-reduced-motion` honored — the global reduced-motion rule in `globals.css` stays. Dark and light themes both meet the bar.
