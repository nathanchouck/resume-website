In everyday speech, the average human has a conversational vocabulary of 3000-5000 words. ChatGPT is commonly cited to have a working vocabulary of just over 100,000 words. However, when generating text, it only uses a small subset of these 100,000 words. Finding an average number for comparison is impossible as everyone talks to ChatGPT or AI models differently, but there is a general consensus that the conversational vocabulary of LLMs is much smaller than human vocabulary. Although hard to predict, there are noticeable trends in AI text, which is how it can be predicted by both humans and computers. Thus emerges a certain style that will never capture the depth of the human condition and soul that comes from every word written. This style of writing is jokingly referred to as the "LinkedIn average," which describes that bland, predictable, sanitized tone that has become a trademark of LinkedIn [1]. This AI tone extends beyond half-baked LinkedIn posts and has now become noticeable in academic and medical writing. 

The number of researchers relying on LLMs for writing is ever-increasing. Words like "delve," "surpass," "boast," and "meticulous" had already seen an increased use around 2020 when generative text models were just being introduced to the public, but saw an unprecedented surge right after the first GPT-3.5 model was made available to the public through a chatbot in 2022. A 2025 study found that out of 135 selected AI-influenced vocabulary words, 103 showed a statistically significant increase (β=0.655,p&lt;0.001) [2]. 

Florida State University published a study in August of 2025 providing statistically significant evidence that human speech and AI vocabulary are beginning to coalesce. They analyzed 22.1 million words from unscripted or otherwise spontaneous spoken language, using references like podcasts. Post-ChatGPT, they discovered that many of the AI-specific buzzwords, for example, "underscore," saw an increase in usage while common synonyms did not see any rise [3]. This is almost expected by basic human psychology. It is well known that humans acquire most of their new vocabulary by reading any form of text, through repeated and natural exposure to words. As more and more people stray from reading books and we see a meteoric rise in AI usage, it is an obvious inevitability that people will begin to use AI buzzwords more frequently. This also isn't something that we can control, as vocabulary processing and word selection is done subconsciously. The homogenization of human vocabulary is an unavoidable tragedy as the global use of AI continues to skyrocket. 

It might seem at first that there is nothing inherently wrong with this, as AI can sometimes use interesting new vocabulary words, but think about it more. Language is a tool humans have used for all of our existence to express individuality, culture, and experience. Every person's vocabulary until this point has been shaped differently by their individual experiences. The words we use convey more than just meaning but also carry the weight of emotion, identity, and perspective that are beyond the literal sense. AI cannot produce this same nuance as it does not have this lived experience. It can only predict what the average of human experience would say, and thus, we all trend towards the mean.

Maybe the greatest danger is not that AI shrinks our vocabulary, but that it is slowly removing the details that lie between. Language has never been valuable because it only conveys meaning, but also because of the imperfections and nuances that get carried in between the lines. These come from individual experience and manifest through unusual metaphors or local dialects and even the occasional awkward sentence, all the product of a life that has never existed before and never will again. 

As our words inexorably converge into what is most probable and statistically preferred, components of the immeasurable human experience will be lost. The world won't fall silent; but it will become uncannily uniform. Our voices will sound different yet we will all speak with the same cadence, rhythm, and tone: guided by humanity's smartest creation.
